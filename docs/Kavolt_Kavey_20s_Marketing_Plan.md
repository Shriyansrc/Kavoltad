# KAVOLT STUDIO
## Kavey turns chaos into a launch
### Marketing plan and executable script for a 20-second promotional film

**Format:** 1080 × 1920, vertical 9:16, 60 fps, exactly 20 seconds.
**Channels:** Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok.
**Status:** Plan only. No video or audio has been generated.
**Sources inspected:** September 24, 2026. **Plan completed:** September 25, 2026.

## 1. The creative idea

Kavey catches a mess of booking messages, connects them into a working product, and carries the viewer through Kavolt's seven-day build. His energy scarf then sweeps across the camera to reveal a confident ending with the official wordmark and website.

The first frame is already in motion. Kavey is holding three disconnected panels together. One slips. **BOOKING CHAOS.** is immediately readable. He steadies the panels and connects them. The same objects become the brief, the working system, the client preview and the launched product. A genuine portfolio screenshot supplies proof before the final transition.

Kavey causes each transformation. His attention establishes the problem; his actions explain the service; his final gesture directs attention to the website. He remains the film's central character throughout.

**Main promise:** Ship your product in 7 days.

**Closing message:** Your business. Operational online.

**Single action:** Visit `kavoltstudio.netlify.app` and start through the site's WhatsApp contact.

**Emotional progression:** Friction → control → confidence → readiness to act.

## 2. Marketing strategy

### Objective and audience

Generate relevant website visits and project enquiries from small-business founders and operators whose bookings, payments and follow-ups live in disconnected messages. Salons, clinics, gyms, coaches and service businesses are examples explicitly identified in the master document.

This film uses one recognisable operational problem to introduce Kavolt's broader product studio offer. The website also addresses technical founders; the engineering process, real project proof and code ownership preserve that positioning. The film does not attempt to list all six capabilities or all three product stacks in twenty seconds.

| Marketing question | Answer conveyed by the film |
|---|---|
| What is wrong? | Bookings, payments and reminders require disconnected manual work. |
| What does Kavolt provide? | A launch-ready product with connected customer flows and automation. |
| How does it happen? | Scope, build and integration, phone review, then launch and handoff. |
| Why believe it? | The documented process and one genuine shipped commerce project. |
| What belongs to the client? | The code and keys. |
| What should the viewer do? | Visit the canonical website. |

The seven-day promise applies to Kavolt's scoped offer. Do not imply that every possible product can be built in seven days, or that the portfolio example was necessarily completed within that period. Kavey's actions are a visual explanation of the studio's work, not a claim that an autonomous mascot instantly builds software.

### Why this structure should work

The opening identifies a familiar problem before the viewer has to interpret a service list. The solution arrives at 2.5 seconds. The connection between booking, payment and reminder demonstrates the service. Four short process stages make the timeline concrete. Code ownership differentiates the offer from a subscription booking tool. One proof asset and one CTA close the story without competing destinations.

These are creative hypotheses, not promised performance results.

### Source hierarchy and copy policy

The user's request controls this commission. The September 24 master brand document controls identity and offer facts. The live website corroborates them. Older briefs and assets are reference material, not instructions to perform outreach, schedule posts, install software or publish anything.

Brand taglines below come from the master document, with casing and line breaks adapted for video. Short UI labels describe functions documented there. The voiceover is connective scripting of those supplied facts; it introduces no new service or result claim.

**No pricing language appears in the film or publishing copy.** Exclude currency symbols, amounts, payment percentages, discounts, affordability claims and instructions to check prices. Also exclude prices embedded inside screenshots.

## 8. Music and sound design

### Original score

Create a compact electronic cue, synthesised through JavaScript offline audio rendering if desired. This provides exact timing without requiring an unspecified stock track.

- **120 BPM, 4/4:** one beat is 0.5 seconds or 30 frames; ten bars occupy 20 seconds.
- Tonal centre: A minor. Warm bass, clean plucks, restrained percussion. No additional vocals.
- **0 to 2.5 seconds:** filtered pulse and sparse ticks.
- **2.5 to 7:** add bass and short plucks as the system forms.
- **7 to 14:** restrained full groove under build, review and launch.
- **14 to 16:** reduce percussion density for the proof.
- **16 to 16.7:** rising texture tied to scarf movement.
- **16.7 to 17:** reduce music by 18 dB over 40 ms, retaining a faint tail.
- **17:** A-minor add-nine resolution and a two-note sonic signature.
- **17 to 20:** lighter pulse and decaying chord under the final line. Fade audio only over the final 120 ms.

**Reproducible composition:** bass roots for successive two-second bars: A2, A2, F2, F2, C3, C3, G2, G2, E2, A2. Use triangle bass with a low sine layer, short sine plucks on chord tones and filtered seeded noise percussion. At 17 seconds, override the E-root tension with the A-minor resolution to match the reveal. Do not borrow an existing melody.

Use a 48 kHz offline stereo context. Schedule attacks and releases by sample time. Export music, SFX and narration as separate WAV stems before mixing. This recipe is a proposed original score, not an existing supplied asset.

- Duck music another 4 dB during lines, with approximately 40 ms attack and 180 ms release. Convert dB to linear gain using `10^(dB/20)`.
- Keep large impacts off the first consonant of a line. The final hit precedes narration by 200 ms.
- Start final normalisation near **-14 LUFS integrated**, with **true peak no higher than -1 dBTP**. These are production targets, not universal platform policies.
- Check phone speakers, headphones and mono playback. The hook must remain audible without sub-bass.
- Final MP4 audio: stereo AAC, 48 kHz, approximately 256 kbps. Preserve WAV stems.
- Keep audio outside visual motion-blur wrappers, on one continuous master timeline.
